## Section

## 12. 3D: depth, stereo, SfM, SLAM, radiance fields and splatting

The rendering and trajectory numbers quoted by these entries come from different captures with different ground truth, so none of them are comparable to each other.

### Depth and stereo

- [Stereo Matching by Training a Convolutional Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.05970) - Where the matching cost stopped being hand-designed while the aggregation around it stayed classical.
- [Pyramid Stereo Matching Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08669) - What a cost volume looks like once the whole stereo pipeline is trained end to end.
- [RAFT-Stereo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07547) - Why iterative refinement replaced a single pass over the full cost volume.
- [Monodepth2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01260) - What one camera can learn about depth from motion alone, and where the scale has to come from instead.
- [Towards Robust Monocular Depth Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01341) - Where the scale and shift invariant protocol for comparing monocular depth across datasets is defined.

### Structure from motion

- [COLMAP](https://github.com/colmap/colmap) - The incremental pipeline most reconstruction work still calibrates against before anything else runs.
- [OpenMVG](https://github.com/openMVG/openMVG) - Where global pose averaging is laid out as separable steps instead of one monolithic solver.
- [Global Structure-from-Motion Revisited](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20219) - Whether global reconstruction matches incremental accuracy once rotation and translation are solved separately.
- [ETH3D Multi-View Evaluation Program](https://github.com/ETH3D/multi-view-evaluation) - Why a reconstruction score means nothing without the tolerance it was computed at.

### Simultaneous localisation and mapping

- [ORB-SLAM3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898) - The feature based system most robotics stacks still start from, with the map handling made explicit.
- [LSD-SLAM](https://github.com/tum-vision/lsd_slam) - What tracking looks like when image intensities replace keypoints and the map stays semi dense.
- [Direct Sparse Odometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02565) - Why photometric calibration of the camera becomes part of the algorithm once features are gone.
- [A Photometrically Calibrated Benchmark For Monocular Visual Odometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02555) - What a capture has to record about lens and exposure before a trajectory error means anything.
- [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869) - Where a learned front end and a dense bundle adjustment back end meet, and what that costs in memory.

### Radiance fields and splatting

- [NeRF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08934) - Where view synthesis became a volume rendering problem solved by fitting one scene at a time.
- [Instant Neural Graphics Primitives](https://github.com/NVlabs/instant-ngp) - Why a hash based input encoding removes most of the training cost without changing the renderer.
- [MultiNeRF](https://github.com/google-research/multinerf) - What unbounded captures and anti-aliasing demand from a radiance field once the scene is not a box.
- [3D Gaussian Splatting](https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting) - What replaces marching along rays once a scene is a set of rasterised primitives rather than a field.

## Claims

### Real-time novel view synthesis at high definition resolution, claimed by 3D Gaussian Splatting

- Fields present: hardware and how many of them, stated as one A6000 in the authors' repository; input resolution; whether preprocessing is inside the timer, answered in the negative because camera calibration runs first and supplies the initial points.
- Fields missing: precision, batch size, framework and version with compile flags, warmup and the statistic reported.
- Verdict: recorded unverified
- Note: Preprocessing is outside the timer, and the preprocessing here is a full structure from motion reconstruction, so the quoted display rate describes the renderer and not the wall-clock cost of showing a new capture. The frame rate is given as a floor rather than a median with a dispersion measure, so it cannot be compared with any timed number produced here.

### View synthesis quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting over its evaluation captures

- Fields present: metric with its full definition; evaluation protocol, including the evaluation script and the held-out view selection shipped in the repository; training data used, in the sense that each scene is fitted on its own images; hardware; the baseline compared against.
- Fields missing: dataset with split and version, because the authors' own documentation states that two of the scenes cannot be openly shared and have to be requested from the original authors.
- Verdict: watchlist
- Note: A score averaged over about a dozen captures, two of which an outsider cannot obtain, is not reproducible by a second group and goes no higher than the watchlist. The entry stays in the core because it rests on the rasteriser and the released implementation, not on the quality table.

### Training a radiance field in seconds, claimed by Instant Neural Graphics Primitives

- Fields present: hardware and how many of them, stated in the repository as one RTX 3090 for every shown result; input resolution for the rendering figure.
- Fields missing: precision, batch size, framework and version with compile flags, warmup and the statistic reported, whether preprocessing and postprocessing are inside the timer.
- Verdict: recorded unverified
- Note: The timer starts after camera poses exist, and obtaining those poses from an unposed capture costs far more than the training time being advertised. Nothing states whether the figure is a median over repeats or a single run.

### Per-scene view synthesis quality reported by NeRF on its synthetic and forward-facing captures

- Fields present: dataset with split and version, as the held-out view protocol and the scene sets are specified and the data is published; metric with its full definition; training data used; the baseline compared against; evaluation protocol.
- Fields missing: model and exact checkpoint, since each scene is a separately fitted model and no per-scene weights are released; hardware and input resolution could not be re-read from the paper, because every publisher and project host was unreachable from this container.
- Verdict: recorded unverified
- Note: The averaged score hides per-scene spread across a scene count small enough that one capture moves the mean, which is the number most downstream comparisons quote. The published scene data is hosted on a consumer file-sharing service, which is a rot risk rather than an evidence gap.

### Zero-shot cross-dataset transfer claimed by Towards Robust Monocular Depth Estimation

- Fields present: model and exact checkpoint, since the releases name each weight file; dataset with split and version for the held-out sets; input resolution and preprocessing; metric with its full definition, which is the scale and shift invariant error; evaluation protocol, including the least squares alignment before scoring; training data used, listed dataset by dataset in the repository.
- Fields missing: hardware; the baseline compared against for the later mixtures rather than the original one.
- Verdict: recorded unverified
- Note: The entry rests on the alignment protocol rather than on any error value, which is the only reason the number's absence costs nothing. The training mixture grew across releases while the name stayed the same, so a quoted error has to name the release as well as the metric.

### Real-time operation claimed by DROID-SLAM

- Fields present: hardware and how many of them, in the sense that the repository states a floor of eleven gigabytes of device memory for inference and four RTX 3090 cards for training; input resolution through the supplied calibration files.
- Fields missing: precision, batch size, framework and version with compile flags, warmup and the statistic reported, whether preprocessing and postprocessing are inside the timer.
- Verdict: recorded unverified
- Note: The front end and the back end can be run in separate processes on separate devices, so a per-frame rate measured in that mode excludes the global bundle adjustment that produces the trajectory being scored. A memory floor above what a robot carries is an evidence fact about the claim, not an aside.

### Metric monocular depth in under a second, claimed by Depth Pro

- Fields present: input resolution and preprocessing; metric with its full definition, including the boundary metrics the authors define; dataset with split and version for the held-out sets; the baseline compared against.
- Fields missing: model and exact checkpoint, contradicted outright because the released weights are a retrained reference implementation that the authors say does not match the paper; hardware, given only as a standard device; training data used, where the real and synthetic mixture is not fully enumerated.
- Verdict: cut
- Note: A checkpoint that is not the measured model makes every other field unusable, since nothing an outsider runs is the thing that was scored. The timing figure also excludes the focal length estimation path that the metric claim depends on.

### A reconstruction F-score is only defined together with its tolerance, per the ETH3D Multi-View Evaluation Program

- Fields present: metric with its full definition; evaluation protocol in full, since the tolerance list, the voxel size used to normalise per volume and the laser beam divergence model are all explicit program arguments; dataset with split and version, through the published ground truth scans.
- Fields missing: none for the protocol itself. Hardware and checkpoint do not apply to a scoring program, and the reconstruction under test stands in their place.
- Verdict: stands
- Note: Because the tolerance is an argument rather than a constant, two papers can quote an F-score on the same scenes and be measuring different things, and a score quoted without its tolerance is unusable. This is the strongest evidence position in the section and the reason the scoring program is cited rather than the benchmark website.

## Benchmark proposal

Depth error from a stereo rig grows with the square of the distance under a fixed disparity error, so a single average depth error over a frame hides exactly where the rig stops working. The measurement generates a synthetic depth field for a fixed focal length and baseline, with most pixels lying on a ground plane so that the near field dominates the pixel count the way it does in a real frame, converts depth to disparity, adds a fixed sub-pixel disparity offset from a seeded generator, inverts back to depth, and reports the median absolute depth error per distance bin alongside the fraction of pixels in each bin and the single frame-wide average. The exponent is then fitted by least squares on the logarithms of the binned medians. 
The claim is settled if the fitted exponent lands near two, if the far bin's median error exceeds the frame-wide average by a factor large enough to change a deployment decision, and if halving the disparity error shifts the curve without moving the exponent; an exponent near one would falsify it. A second pass warps a seeded texture by the ground truth disparity to make a rectified pair and matches it with an integer block search, to show that disparity quantisation alone reproduces the far-field blow-up with no noise model at all. Pillow writes the texture and the error maps, numpy does the rest, nothing is fetched at run time, and the quick mode runs a coarse grid in well under ten seconds.
